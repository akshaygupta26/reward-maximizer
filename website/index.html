Privacy

Private by architecture,
not by promise.

Most rewards tools want your logins on their servers. Reward Maximizer has no servers. Everything lives in your browser's local storage — there is literally nowhere for your data to go.

🔒 What "local-only" means

  • No accounts. Nothing to sign up for, nothing to leak in a breach.
  • No analytics. No tracking pixels, no telemetry, no "product insights".
  • No cloud. Your offers never leave your device, period.
  • Open source. Every line is inspectable on GitHub — don't trust, verify.

🔑 Why it asks for each permission

  • Storage — keeps your synced offers on your device.
  • Tabs — detects when you're on a merchant site to show an alert.
  • Notifications — reminds you about offers before they expire.
  • Alarms — lightweight housekeeping for background sync state.
  • Site access — reads offers on your bank's pages and shows alerts while you shop. Nothing else.
Honest monetization

How is it free?

Fair question. Two ways, both disclosed up front:

1. Referral links. Some shopping-portal links in the app (like Rakuten) are referral links. They're always clearly labeled, and using them costs you nothing — it just sends a small thank-you our way if you sign up.

FAQ

Questions, answered straight

Is it really free? What's the catch?
Yes, free — no premium tier, no paywalled features. It's supported by clearly-labeled referral links (e.g. Rakuten) and optional tips. The catch is there isn't one: the extension is open source, so anyone can verify there's no funny business.
Does it see my banking passwords?
No. You log in on your bank's own website exactly as usual — the extension never asks for, sees, or stores your credentials. It only reads the offer listings on pages you already have open.
Which banks and cards are supported?
American Express, Chase, Citi, Capital One, Discover, Bank of America, and US Bank, plus Rakuten for portal stacking. Support is best-effort and improves with each release — banks change their sites often.
Will it slow down my browser?
No. It does nothing until you sync or visit a merchant site — no background crypto miners here, just a lightweight check when you shop.
Who makes this?
An independent developer who got tired of leaving Amex Offers and Chase Offers unclaimed. It's a side project, built in the open — bug reports and contributions are welcome on GitHub.
How do I get support?
Email rewardmaximizer@gmail.com — a human (the developer) reads everything.
